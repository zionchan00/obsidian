- The problem with traditional university is that most students are here only for the grades, learning only because it is expected of them to do so. As a result discussions in class cannot help but be low quality, as most people have not done the requisite readings, let alone actually understood them. Teachers often are stuck only at explaining the text to the class as a result, turning what should have been a time for quality discussion into a sparknotes summary of the reading. Large class sizes prevent teachers from actually engaging with any one student well. University has been a very lonely experience for me.
- The Catherine Project solves these issues firstly by offering no legible reward for education other than the wisdom, friendship, and love gained, filtering out unserious students and attracting those motivated by a genuine curiosity for the great books. Secondly, class sizes are small enough that a dialectic can form between members and tutors, with every member of the class feeling that they matter.
- I hope to gain a correction of my prejudice that modern understanding is greater than the understanding of humans in the past, that it is unlikely that the great thinkers of the past knew more about the world than modern thinkers do. I ​think studying these ancient texts with experts would increase the quantity and quality of the insights unearthed from these texts, further correcting my prejudice than if I had studied these great works solo.
