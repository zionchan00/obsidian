The answer is hidden in the explanatory power of the dollar and the renminbi. Why is the Chinese sovereign more willing to commoditize the commercial frontier than the American sovereign? It is down to financial markets and the mass populations, here and there, subservient.

Where is wealth stored in the average Chinese household? Since the end of the Cultural Revolution, it is no longer stuffed under mattresses or buried in some field outside the village until the Red Guards pass. Today, the way that Chinese wealth is stored is fully modern: banks, checking accounts, a system of checking, savings, and withdrawal that looks not unlike your own despite being a closed capital account.

But the composition of that household wealth is different, which goes a long way in explaining why the Chinese party-state is less compelled to prop up the stock market. The Chinese see themselves as a democracy, just of a different sort. A state ultimately answers to its people, whether or not it is formally democratic. _Panem et circenses_, both being superabundant. In ensuring prosperity, the Chinese state responds to a set of incentives quite different than ours. Their attitudes about the governance of machine intelligence, the level of state capital expenditure, the adaptive interference of regulators and censors: it all is downstream of whether such policies advance the primary imperative of the modern state: wealth preservation and creation.

Valuations in China are increasingly determined by the market, but without the trillion-dollar exuberance of those in America. There is also active consent, it seems, from the Chinese labs to this paradigm. In a leaked four-hour investor call, DeepSeek CEO Liang Wenfeng candidly described his strategy for the firm’s commercialization and his plan for an enduring edge. Liang explicitly stated that DeepSeek’s models would remain open-weight for the foreseeable future. Even more shocking to American capital, DeepSeek would not aim to maximize profits against all other interests. He would instead guide the firm, in which Liang holds the majority of shares, toward what he termed a “reasonable profit.”[71](https://commanding-heights.com/#n-71) What would the American landscape look like if the investors and financiers who underwrote it believed that AI would return just a “reasonable profit,” rather than extracting maximum shareholder value from as large a surface area as possible?

As the Chinese continue their regime of price compression, American labs will be forced to finance the _n_th training run regardless of changes to their pricing power. The alternative, letting simple competition slow the rate of progress, would devalue the artifact too much. No, training expenditure must continue long after the ordinary returns on invested capital have begun to deteriorate. New financing mechanisms will be employed to keep the party going far after the value of the original expenditure is written down. We are watching the cascade today, from operating cash flow, then to IG bonds, devolving and devolving until finally coming to rest against the backstop of the sovereign.

In the end, the sovereign will truly announce itself by financing the continued diffusion of machine intelligence. Debt as we think of it will not meet the needed expenditure given the rigid structure of principal, maturity and coupon payments in the face of open-weight releases that impair the collateral. The final funding gap will be filled by instruments subscribed to the artifact itself, laundered out of short-termism, desiring to ride the ascent without regard for the calendar, an unshakable faith in the reshaping.
