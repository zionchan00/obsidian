- The things I took from TLP:
	- 1). You are what you actually do. Conversely you are not what you tell yourself or anyone else you are. Football players play football, musicians make music, students study, accountants keep books. Nerds are into obscure stuff. Whatever it is, DO IT.
	- 2). Anything that keeps you from changing your actual behavior is not only a dead end, but a defense mechanism for bad habits. Reading about and thinking about and planning for and buying equipment for your change is defending you from changing. As is posting about it on social media. And talking about it.
	- 3). (This isn't direct, but I found it extremely helpful). Measure yourself daily on the things that matter to you. If you want to be fit, count sit ups or push-ups or time a run. If you're not improving, something is wrong. The same would go for other things. If you want to be a family person, keep track of how much family time you spend. If you want to be nicer, keep track of it. If you want to be kinder, or braver, or more frugal, keep track of it.
	- 4). The mind is really really good at tricking itself and propaganda machines have taken full advantage of the mind tricking itself. As such, pay very close attention to what the desires are. Start with yourself-- what do you want to be true? What does society want to be true? What does the author want to be true? What are the images branding your news as being for?
