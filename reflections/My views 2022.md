- The Good Life
	- The ultimate end of life is happiness, which is dependent on meaning (feeling valued). The difference between meaning and happiness is that things that give us meaning can make us temporarily unhappy, but in the long-term make us much happier.  It's also possible that being valued can not be conducive to long-term happiness, because it depends on what you're being valued for (is it a good act) and by who (is it a good person). We're looking for healthy sorts of meaning. Sources of meaning
		- What we create
		- The people around us. The strongest and closest relationships are the ones with the closest match in values.
			- by match in values, i mean what we believe to be important in life, what one should spend their time on. My values are that one should be oriented towards a life of self-discovery (learning about what one loves, one's weaknesses and strengths are, how to become a better human) and world discovery (learning about how the world works across any field). But more specifically, I'm closest with people who are meta-level thinkers
				- the reason that a match in values creates the strongest/closest relationships is because it creates INTIMACY, which makes the relationship feel closer and more enjoyable overtime > you only want to hangout MORE overtime. when we live our lives in a similar way, when we care about similar things, we feel that this person is similar to us, and that means we will understand one another, so we can open up and talk about what we BOTH WANT TO EXACTLY, with no need for alteration
				- Increased time spent with one another + longevity = more memories from the past to refer to, which makes the relationship have stronger emotional intensity and longevity
					- But I'm not sure if this is ALWAYS a good thing, you can stay friends with people purely about reminiscing on the past which isn't good. So this is a fine line, and reminds me of a good heuristic - how much of a relationship is spent reminiscing on the past? if it's a lot, odds are it isn't a very strong relationship
				- When we open ourselves up to others, we let their opinions and views shape who we are. Relationships with intimacy tend to get more intimate because we tend to converge personalities by affecting one another's development
			- While relationships that feel the most fun do so on the basis of similarity, the danger is that who we currently are may be evil or suboptimal as I was in the past. As a result I attracted people who would prevent me from becoming a better person
	- Maybe we can distinguish between long-term and short-term happiness too. There are sources of short-term happiness which are amazing. - What was the conflict that I faced in september
	- In September I was constantly thinking about what I was not (not an expert in any area yet, my friends aren't particularly deep or philosophical beings) instead of what I was (someone who already has these interests, someone who does have a small number of philosophical people around them who are good enough to give me quality feedback and hence my feeling of having 'outgrown' them is false), creating sadness from the fact that I was always distant from my goal and my life always seeming bad. While my life always could be improved, such a mentality creates permanent sadness from moving forward based on 'negative' instead of 'positive'. There will always be something that I'm not, and therefore more reasons to continue living unhappily. Instead, it's better to focus on an aspect of yourself that you already have and increase how much of your identity it occupies. In doing so you're focused on how much you enjoy the activity and therefore how you should have more fun
