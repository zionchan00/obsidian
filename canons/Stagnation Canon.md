- Turchin, Andrey Korotayev, etc. concentrate on what is best called 'land-power' based political formations, They are interested in:  
1. Highlighting demographic pressures.  
2. Concentrating on the Rise and Fall of Land Powers.  
3. Agrarian production; its growth and decline.  
They find assorted cycles in their studies.  
  
Korotayev and Turchin write excellent books for this school of thought. The historical school they belong to, Cliodynamics, used to have a great deal of translated Russian authors out there on the 'net. Since I don't read Russian, it was very helpful. There are many excellent historical schools out there trying to understand the broad sweep of world history. Besides cliodynamics, have a look at macro sociology (Randall Collins, Theda Skocpo, Charles Tilly), world system theory (Andre Gundar Frank, Immanuel Wallerstein), and the good old civilizational school that began waaay back with Spengler and Toynbee. I much prefer universal history to particular history.  
  
Another group of historians and sociologists (Modelski does a lot of work here), btw, studies what can be termed 'sea-powers', they:  
1. Focus on Trading States.  
2. Maritime Activities.  
3. Economic Growth Pulsations (k-waves).  
They too find cycles.
