**Imaginal exposure work**: Visualize romantic scenarios that typically trigger your shutdown - someone saying they love you, making future plans, expressing how much you mean to them. Notice when your body contracts or you start dissociating. Practice breathing and staying present with the discomfort instead of fleeing mentally.


**Work with the underlying terror**: What specifically about romantic obligation feels threatening? Loss of autonomy? Fear of disappointing someone? Being truly known? Sit with these fears in meditation/therapy rather than avoiding them.

**Study your past shutdowns**: Journalø about specific moments when partners' investment felt burdensome rather than beautiful. What was the somatic experience? Chest tightness? Numbness? Mental fog? Learn to recognize your early warning signs.

**Practice receiving in general**: Let friends help you with things. Notice if you minimize their efforts or feel uncomfortable with their generosity. The romantic version is just an amplified version of this pattern.

**Attachment-focused therapy**: Work specifically on dismissive-avoidant patterns. EMDR can help if there's trauma around early caretaking that created the defensive pattern.

**Somatic work with imagined scenarios**: Visualize someone loving you deeply and practice staying in your body instead of floating away into analysis mode.

**Somatic Experiencing** (Peter Levine) - tracks autonomic nervous system responses to help you notice when your body contracts or expands around emotional stimuli. Good for learning to feel feelings as they arise rather than after the fact.

**Focusing** (Eugene Gendlin) - teaches you to sense the "felt sense" of situations in your body. You'd learn to literally feel the weight/texture/quality of someone's investment in you as bodily sensation.

**Sensorimotor Psychotherapy** - integrates talk therapy with body awareness, helping you notice how attachment patterns show up as physical tension or numbness.

**Basic mindfulness of sensations** - but the secular, clinical kind, not the commodified wellness version. Jon Kabat-Zinn's approach.

You have **two extreme modes**: complete boundary dissolution (work, some social situations) where you over-empathize to the point of abandoning valid positions, or total boundary rigidity (romantic relationships) where you create an impermeable wall to avoid that dissolution.

The romantic shutdown isn't about fear of intimacy per se - it's a defensive overcorrection against your tendency to lose yourself through excessive empathy. You're so afraid of the workplace pattern (dropping your valid position because you start feeling sorry for the person who wronged you) that in romantic contexts you swing to the opposite extreme: total emotional disconnection to preserve autonomy.
