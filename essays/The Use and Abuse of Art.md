- When we think of art we should ask "What set of conditions does it accompany and illuminate? What sort of mind does it fulfill or edify"
- Never has art dominated our lives so much. Headphones are popped in as soon as one steps outside the house, screens power on and Netflix with it as soon as we come home, and. In these 6 lectures, Barzun argues despite the prevalence of art, never have we been so unthinking about what its raisen d'etre may be, whether it fulfills it or not, and hence whether art is good or bad. Specifically three harms are introduced in the modern conception of Art
	- Firstly that Art is harmless, automatically a good which must be revered
	- Second that society does not even try to think what a raisen d'etre for Art might be
- He concerns himself with the Renaissance (1400-1600), the Romantic period (1790-1840), the turn of the century (1890-1914), and contemporary (1920-1973, when the lectures were given)
- The Enlightenment's reason took down the place of religion > but man still had a spiritual side which it wanted fulfilled > Rousseau discovered divinity in nature > Schiller and the Romanticist's wanted to have art take the place of religion by capturing the divinity and beauty in nature
	- art becoming religion made artists believe they were no longer descriptors of society but prescriptors of society, just like real religious prophets
		- This increased the power of the artist and led to them being believed to be first geniuses, then creators
		- As art became critical of society it began to distance itself from society
	- Combined with technology which lowered the cost of production and increased accessibility of art, the status of art was raised and overtime society praised art uncritically. Hilariously the bourgeois and the masses consumed the same works which hurled invectices against them.
	- The artists never got the change which they wished for. Barzun makes the amazing observation that no matter the time period, artists always bitch about the bourgeois as the cause of societal ills. The abuses against the bourgeois started in the 12th century when they first appeared and continue until the 20th century. How likely is it that the bourgeois have remained unchanged through all this time? Perhaps the arguments against the bourgeois have changed overtime (this'll have to be checked).
	- The consequence of a lack of common ground between the artist <> non-artist is an inability for consumers to properly critique what the artist has created. We look at the weird contraptions in museums and don't even know where to begin when analyzing them. The pieces are simply too absurd. But the artist is laughing all the way to the bank. They feel proud of how subversive, how shocking, how innovative they are.
		- But if Art is only merely interesting, it can't push us towards revolution (pg. 17). This means the artists have gave up on revolution and here are only to satirize our taste, it has self-destructed
- Barzun believes the project was doomed to fail from the beginning because art can never replace religion. He believes art is incapable of escaping society. Art is stuck forever describing what we in society know.not only influences society but society also influences art
- Throughout the book (pages 11, ) I get the hint that Barzun is against the increasing definition of art, what he would likely deem a 'lowering of standards'.
- We must then ask ourselves the question of what the use of art is. To me the last life-affirming piece of art that I witnessed was Ayn Rand's Fountainhead. Barzun's critique is very true in the visual arts. In music, pop music throughout time seems to resist the decadence and lost of life-energy which other art forms have. My hypothesis is that music, as a democratic art form which for the most part has escaped the deattachment from society that visual artists have experienced, has remained grounded and still communicates in a manner understandable by the masses.
