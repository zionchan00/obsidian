Tier 1 VC's usually claim to have a 'hit rate' of 45-65%. At the end of the year they review all the 'must invest' deals that took place during the year and see how many they missed. Fortunately for them, they've seen 70-100% of them.

If you're interested in early-stage startups at all, this should setoff alarm bells in your head. These 'must invest' deals are designated so because the teams are considered to be top decile talent (Tier 1 VC's get a lot of practice spotting talent) and are targeting markets that are obviously large (which means the TAM math is easy and hence it becomes easy to believe that the financial reward for success is large). 

Why do 35-55% of these great teams fail? With so much brain power in the room, through discussion 
Because most companies make smart people dumber, and you have to actually intentionally design your organization for this to not be true. The reason why I believe this is true is because you'd naturally think that so much brain power in the room SHOULD be able to be aware that a) whatever they're working on is not working b) they should pivot to find a new source of spend to tap into.

Everyone says they want employees with 'high agency', who are 'creative', 'innovative', but few know how to design an organization geared towards these people. No matter their intelligence, most of the ideas these employees generate won't work. This is especially true in new organizations, where the most pressing problems have very little public information on how to solve them, and your team will have to try multiple solutions before figuring it out.
![[Pasted image 20250504101452.png]]
When I choose to work with someone, I ask myself, how would they react if I had a hit rate of 1/100 on an unsolved problem?

My favourite test for whether someone has a grip loose enough for a high-experimentation culture is by asking them to make a choice in a domain with naturally high variance, and watching their reaction when the outcome lands below expectations. Does their body understand that in these domains, the default is failure? When they give a rational explaination of the failed outcome, do they try to Chesteron's fence the opposing view?

I feel comfortable posting this and letting future candidates know this is what I test for because it's impossible for people who fail this test to hide how they truly feel.
