- Thoughts
	- What activities do you want to use it for, and what do you not want to use it for? Anything iterative, large-search spaced, brute forceable, with a large TAM is likely a good application for AI to end-to-end it (bio/math). Anything where consumption of it is costly (e.g investment from the consumer takes a long time) is where likely you want to make sure it's perfect and have a human in the loop checking every detail.
		- It might be good at solving math, but it doesn't seem to be good at meta-gaming type activities. This is when what's valuable changes overtime, making it hard for a model to track success, AND where the cost of making a mistake is high. An example of an activity that AI is good at is mathematics research - the barriers to entry to do a good job are purely information (as research is published online, and the tacit knowledge of researchers is made available for engines to query through the papers) + compute (both money and access to it); the problem is difficult mostly because any specific approach takes a very long time to prove out, meaning you can't try that many combinations of approaches, so the search space is large; you can also try millions of things and justify the cost because the cost is purely financial/compute. This is what'll be first automated, and why I think math is a great doamin.
		  
		  We can contrast this to anything related to a company's brand. A company could try A/B testing billboards/website/product, and AI can generate these and run experiments. But if you want significant data, you'll need scale, and the more people you run these on the higher the chances that you'll leave someone with a bad impression.
