There are also non-empiricist takes on classical theory (e.g primitives of social, cultural, and functional)
What would an inferential verison of classical theory look like?
Strengths: concept acquisition (acquire the components to learn the concept), categorization, epistemic justification, analytic inference (smith is a bachelor > smith is a man, confirmed by concept of bachelor having concept of man), reference determination
Criticisms
- Plato's problem. it's difficult to break concepts into sensory experiences (e.g break lie into speaker, affirmation, negation - are they any closer to sensory level?) + definitions are debatable (beauty, justice, truth - could just be that definitional structure does not exist)
- Psychology experiments are unable to account for definition through conceptual structure
	- classical theory can adapt [[inferential model]] OR definitions are 'chunked'
- Quine's denial that analyticity is possible at all
- Kripke proves description can't be used to fix referent bc we can be wrong/ignorant about correct properties of referent so our description is inaccurate (but if you believe descriptionism and two people have diff symptoms for measles, then it's two diff diseases), we can imagine other worlds where description is different yet same referent
	- Kripke/Putnam propose we find referent through causal-historical examples
- Another critique is situations where it's unclear whether an object falls under a concept. But this can be countered by the compositional concepts being fuzzy themselves, allowing for indeterminacy
- classical theory can't explain typicality bc it doesn't have a model for which compositional concepts are more or less important, nor does it have an explanation for how some objects could 'better' or worse embody a concept, e.g shouldn't all examples be good if they fall under the constituent concepts?
