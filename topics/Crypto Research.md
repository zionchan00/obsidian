	- The drivers of demand for stablecoin
		- Stablecoins are a worthwhile study because, like all other attempts to design a currency, they raise the question of what gives a currency value or in another frame, what are the [[drivers of demand for a currency]]
			- Payment - the more entities accept the acceptance of a currency as a method of payment for goods/services, the higher the demand for a currency
			- Store of value - a currency is a good store of value when the price is low in volatility, which decreases the chances that your savings will be wiped. Ideally, it's also hard to steal and able to escape physical decay.
			- Intrinsic value - a currency can be worth the amount that 'backs' it. I.e if you have $100 each dollar could be exchanged for a ton of gold, then each dollar is worth a ton of gold. I'm not sure what my old view is, but One thing Defi has hammered into me is the framing of value as an exchange; the value of something is dependent on what you can trade it for, the conversion of one measurement of value to another way to measure value. 
			- Investment asset - the belief that the demand for a currency in the future will be higher than it is today either because a) demand will increase such that demand is greater than supply or b) supply will decrease such that supply is less than demand will drive demand for the currency today. This is something most users of a currency don't think of every day but drives much of the demand for a currency in crypto
		- All stablecoin demand is driven by all of these factors, but the last one the least intrinsically. If stable coins are able to maintain their peg, they're an excellent means of payment and store of value because currency users won't have to fear losing money on a transaction or their savings from price volatility. The third driver is how stablecoins separate themselves in terms of design. One choice is to do what $USDC has done and be 100% backed by an actual US dollar - for every USDC token that exists, there is $1 USD to be exchanged for it. New USDC is created only when a US dollar is given to the organization
		- The problem is that the USD has to be stored in an off-chain entity, which clashes with the ethos of crypto. So developers have created 'algorithmic' stablecoins, which are backed by a basket of cryptocurrencies and sometimes real dollars too, which employ the usage of algorithms to adjust the basket as prices fluctuate. These algorithmic stablecoins tend to be 'overcollateralized' to compensate for price volatility, meaning there is more than $1 of backing for every token.

	- Prevailing narrative. Cryptocurrency is important because we're building a financial system which is better than the old one because
		- transparency from all transactions being on-chain which leads to
			- better risk management from players having a more accurate understanding of reality (bad plays still happen from misunderstanding whitepapers and code tho)
			- evil in traditional finance is caused by corrupt humans in power. evil in crypto comes from governance attacks/hacks
		- decentralization from token-governance and all code on-chain which leads to
			- forkability (anyone can copy-paste your code to start a competitor) + apps can't gatekeep different applications from using their services (permissionless APIs) = faster rate of innovation + intense competition = better end products
				- The problem with this is that the improved products may remain 'stuck' only in the world of crypto. I.e Uniswap vs all the Uniswap clones creates better decentralized AMMs, but that isn't world-positive if all that is traded on the AMMs amounts to zero-sum wealth reshuffling
			- + consensus mechanisms which remove the need for KYC/risk-management middlemen = 'traditional financial systems margin is DeFi's opportunity
