- My Lux Story
	- It's rare to get an internship offer at a brand-name early-stage venture fund, it's even harder to do so as an undergrad, and it's unheard of as a Canadian. Young people are unlikely to possess the qualities needed to find and win investments: logical rigour to cut through uncertainty (thesis-driven investing) and a network of founders (human-driven investing). When I first discovered Lux Capital at the beginning of 2020, I knew I wanted to work alongside these people immediately. I loved how intellectually curious everyone at the firm was and how mission-driven they were. Two barriers were in the way, however. Firstly they invested in the frontier of science and as my friends loved to remind me, I bombed grade 10 science in high school, so I lacked the knowledge needed to be useful to them. Second of all, I had no network into the firm or Silicon Valley broadly.  
  
	I broke the problem down and realized I could make this happen still. The science was learnable and if I demonstrated I was capable of doing the work even before I had the job, I would derisk the introductions and people would view introducing me to the firm as a positive for themselves. If I still didn't get a chance after this, a workplace that would not reward my initiative would not be a good fit for me anyway. I picked healthcare and biology as the verticals I would dive into and after many textbooks and papers, I created two pieces of research. People around the firm saw my work and were willing to make introductions and at the end of the year, I received an offer to join the firm for four months.  
  
	There was no precedent for my dream and the odds were low but I told myself I was going to make this work no matter what. The story you just read demonstrates what I bring to the team - a love of learning, strong logic, and scrappiness. I have the right experience for this role from planning go-to-market for a newco at Alphabet and exploiting my understanding of competitive landscapes as a venture investor. I would love to meet all of you and learn about how you're solving a problem that matters to me; give me the chance to prove I can do the job and I will show you the difference I'll make in business outcomes and how energizing it is to work with me.


Why healthcare
	There are three reasons I want to work in healthcare. First, I enjoy working in industries with a large number of stakeholders, as it ups the creativity of the solutions. No better place to do this than healthcare - the provider is different from the payer and the payer is different from the end-user, how do you create a win for most of them to adapt your product? Second, improving human wellbeing matters; being mission-driven and working with mission-driven people is non-negotiable for me. I am lastly interested in working in American healthcare to better understand how to save both American and Canadian healthcare from cost disease. Given that the American system provides more opportunities for startups to grow, America's the best place to identify how tech can create better outcomes at lower prices - that's the power of technology!  
	I want to help underserved communities because they are the demographic that both needs new solutions the most but are also one of the most difficult to solve for. I love the challenge and the knowledge that if we get this right, we are going to create one of the largest deltas in outcomes possible.
