		- 1. With few transistors (let's say 10):  
		    You can only place one puzzle piece every 10 seconds.  
		    Completing a 1000-piece puzzle would take about 2.8 hours.
		2. With more transistors (let's say 1000):  
		    You can place 100 puzzle pieces every second.  
		    The same 1000-piece puzzle would now take only 10 seconds.
		3. With even more transistors (let's say 1 million):  
		    You could theoretically place 100,000 pieces per second.  
		    You could complete multiple 1000-piece puzzles in less than a second.

			- Marketplaces pre-internet
				- The big marketplaces were where people paid attention: physical spaces where people lived and shopped (brick and mortar, literal grocery stores, literal malls, downtown areas), media (TV/radio/magazines/cinema). 
				- If you wanted to rent a home, or you wanted to get a taxi, you had to rely on the old gatekeepers: newspapers, TVs, physical posters
					- This was whether you wanted to find entertainment, education, and things to buy/sell
			- What did the Internet do to change this?
				- First, the Internet digitized many of these goods. This made them 'non-rivalrous', which means multiple copies can exist with no extra cost of producing another version. We say that the marginal cost of production has dropped to 0 - make it once, and you can make tons of copies for basically no cost and earn pure profit
					- What was the business impact of all of this? First, this gave Internet companies the ability to offer lower prices to consumers and hence a better bargain for them vs the same product being sold in-real life. E.g Spotify, Netflix
				- Second, the Internet allows for maximum choice when you're shopping. You aren't limited by what can physically be shown to you, you can instead have an 'infinite' store because your screen can simualte an infinite number of things. The only limit to what can be shown is what's available on a website
					- What's the business impact? Firstly, digital stores could beat traditional retailers by offering way more choice for you to pick from. Second, in the long-run digital stores also had more methods to capture what is that you liked through algorithms. Amazon, Uber, Instacart - these are 'real-world marketplaces'
