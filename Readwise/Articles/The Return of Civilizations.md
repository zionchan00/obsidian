## Highlights
- There is an argument that the return of the civilization state was prepared by Jewish thinkers in the 19th century. Leo Strauss and others learned to doubt the promise of liberalism in the school of Zionism. The liberal state failed to ensure the safety and dignity of European Jews. It failed to ensure their physical survival, but it also seemed incapable of delivering on its promise that Jews could be fully themselves in a liberal society, free of the fear of making themselves different. We can detect in these 19th-century debates the germ of the idea of Israel as a civilization state, even if Theodor Herzl, the father of Zionism, remained a very imperfect guide.
