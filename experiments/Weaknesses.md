### **Explicit Pre-mortem Exercise** (forced cognitive reset):

- Clearly state your current scenario (the one you're stuck on).
    
- Then, explicitly assume this scenario already failed badly.
    
- Write in detail about exactly _how_ and _why_ it failed.
    
    - What assumptions did you incorrectly hold?
        
    - What critical factors did you neglect?
        
    - Who acted differently than anticipated, and why?
        
- This forces your brain to abandon its fixated view by making it explicitly untenable.
    

### ② **Structured Scenario Trees** (confront cognitive rigidity head-on):

- Draw out an explicit branching tree, forcing at least **five** plausible scenarios from any decision point.
    
- Each scenario must explicitly have at least two further branches.
    
- Even if your brain resists ("but this wouldn't happen"), do it anyway. Force yourself through this discomfort explicitly.

**Neurological approaches:**

- Develop scenarios in a completely different physical environment from your normal one (neurological state-dependent memory can trap thinking)
- Write with your non-dominant hand when brainstorming alternatives (activates different neural pathways)
- Use "sleep incubation" - pose the problem before bed, keep paper nearby for morning insights
- - Deploy the "regret minimization framework" - project yourself forward to age 80 and consider which choice would minimize regret
- Practice "pre-mortems" on your own decisions - imagine a future where your decision led to failure and articulate why
- - Engage in "perspective-taking" exercises where you verbally steelman their position before responding
