- To me philosophy is the search for true being underneath the surface of the forms which it takes. Whether we know it or not, we are all philosophers in all of our dealings with other humans, but especially in love.
- Never are we unable to guarantee that the other intends to follow what they have communicated, that the truth is in the surface of things, that what is expressed is authentic to what is felt. The problem of authenticity becomes even more strained from the weight of the promises that lovers make to one another. Til' death do us part, but we cannot plug our ears from the stories of these vows being broken, from true love proven false. We try to surmount the problem in altering the form our declarations take. A hundred roses instead of one, love letters a thousand words instead of a hundred. A hole of doubt can always be poked in each expression and unfortunately the problem of authentic love has been experienced so many times that our solutions are rarely original. Often our acts are dictated by the stories we consume, herding us towards clichés. It is a tragedy that every lover must resort to the words 'I love you', words which have been polluted so many times by those with feelings which did not match, yet because they name what we wish to express, always we return to them.
- It is this uncertainty that leads us to desire that our partner prove their love to us by materializing that which is inside of us to the sensed world. We want to test their dedication, see how much they will give up, and in comparison to what they are willing to sacrifice we guess at our worth to them. Hence Patricia, and as Michel says many a female's, admiration for the Romeo and Juliet story where Romeo chooses to die rather than live without Juliet.

- Breathless serves as a great testing ground for the problem of authenticity because of its pathologically lying protagonist, Michel, and the danger to society he presents. Even as outsiders who have a perspective which none of the characters can replicate, we are only able to tell whether Michel is lying or not when he covers up his crimes. The veracity of his personal life - that is which we are unable to confirm. He lies so smoothly that one must wonder whether he lies because he is a criminal or is he a criminal because he lies? The line between Michel the criminal who lies for the sake of the job and Michel the real person is always blurred because he does not separate his criminals from his civilian life. He willingly mixes both, robbing his acquaintances and turning civilians into conspirators. When an old flame offers him five-hundred as a loan he respectfully declines, before taking all of her cash while she changes her clothes.
- Even though the truth regarding him is elusive, Michel cannot help but long for certainty that Patricia loves him. He signals to her his desire for a girl who will not 'stop halfway' when he tells her the story about a couple who were recently arrested in which the girl reacted to her partner's confession that he is a criminal by joining him in his crimes. This is his test; will you risk your civilian life and its freedom for me?
- The proud trumpets and sax from the swing soundtrack combine with the shaky handheld camera movements from the running cameraman creates a fast-paced energy throughout, restlessness, as if everyone is always running away from the broken promises made. The reason why can be found in both Michel and Patricia. Michel, who is willing to violate all the laws and customs of society according to the caprices of his will, a man who's only absolutes are found within him, can not be trusted at the risk of change. Those who make the mistake of trusting him, from acquaintances to strangers in public spaces, risk losing items ranging from cash to cars to consciousness. Patricia, who throughout the film mentions that she wishes not to like Michel because she wants to protect her freedom. To the freedom loving modern love, a restriction of choice which denies us from all but our other, is an area to be avoided, a thing to be pushed back until the biological clock tells us we have no choice. Perhaps with improved science even this can be solved and truly the problem of commitment can be pushed back indefinitely.
- The problem of authenticity runs not only between humans but within humans. How can we know if what we feel is what we really feel? Patricia uses the same logic to solve the problem of inter-human authenticity; she will test herself just as she wishes to test Michel by whether she will betray him to the police. She spends time with him and commits crimes with him to make sure whether she loved him or not. She reasons that alas, because she was able to call the police, a 'mean thing', hence she does not truly love him. The mistake she makes is that the search for authenticity is doomed to fail from the beginning. The creation of a true self independent of all other influence has nothing to tie itself down to. She first begins with figuring out whether she likes him or not, and when she realizes she loves him, she must then test whether she loves him or not. Never can the feelings we have be relied upon.
- The film ends with Michel shot by the police, hobbling away until he collapses. Patricia stands alongside the police above his barely-alive body. She covers her mouth as one would when they are about to pule. Her expression when she looks at him are not tender nor forlorn but stone cold and judgmental. Michel's death is quite comedic in that he refused the gun offered to him by his friend yet when the police arrives the gun is tossed to him anyways, cranking up the confrontation which leads him to be shot. When the dying Michel utters his last words, 'makes me want to puke', is he condemning his friend for dooming him to die? The whole world? Those who stand above him? Patricia only? Both of their fantasies are subverted. The girl he thought was the Bonnie to his Clyde betrays him to the authorities. He dies just like Romeo does but his choice against running away was not because he could not bear bear being locked up in a concrete 6x8 cell away from her but because he is tired.
- Michel's last words are lost in translation; the policeman must mediate his final words and perhaps he distorts them. Still at the end they are out of touch, unable to guarantee that they are understood by one another for who they really are.
