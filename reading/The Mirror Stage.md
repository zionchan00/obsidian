- Lacan draws from the fact that when both a baby and a chimpanzee see themselves in the mirror, even though the latter demonstrates higher instrumental intelligence in its greater mastery over tools, only the former recognizes itself in the mirror from the synchronized movements of its reflection
- The mirror self the baby perceives is a [[gestalt]], it is also psychologically outside the baby (in relation to its interior world), that seizes the baby in that as a gestalt. It allows the baby to realize itself as an I but the baby sees it as superior to its real self because the baby's body is not fully developed yet in its motor skills. In contrast the baby sees the perfect self in the mirror, recognizes that they will gain more motor control in the future and hence a temporal gap exists between the two, and  pursuit of that Ideal-I begins.
