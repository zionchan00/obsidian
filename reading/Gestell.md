- Heidegger's word for what is the 'essence' of technology. it refers to way of viewing the world, a way which MAYBE came even before anything which we call 'technology' was invented, in which we see everything as a resource to be manipulated, an input into some form of an equation to be transformed according to our desires to what we want. 
- We could think about this 'by itself', in which we see the nature around us as resources to be harnessed, as fuel for our machines. What makes this analysis even more interesting is the introduction of markets, which increase the set of goods which fall under this worldview. Our home can be an 'input' into a transformation which trades it for money. As markets grow and more $ values are thrown onto everything, as long as we follow the gestell of technology, everything becomes mobilizable and tradable. What do we prevent from holding onto this? 
- Heidegger seems to me to be on the mark. The less important question is how did we fall into this worldview, the more interesting question is what other worldviews can we track, and the most important question is how do we get what comes after? What is going to be my [[theory of change]]?
