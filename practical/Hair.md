Yeah DHT is certainly a huge part of the equation, but we know that the balding scalp contains high levels of localized DHT. And DHT is part of an inflammatory response that studies have been found promote TGFb1 activity, the protein mainly responsible for promoting tissue fibrosis. So what appears to be happening (and studies confirm that fully bald scalps are mainly scar tissue) is this sequence of events. And we know that hair follicules die in scar tissue. The other thing about pattern baldness is that it does happen in a very predictable pattern right above the area where the temporalis and occipitalis attach to the skull. Another study found that the tension distribution of the scalp lines up nearly perfectly with the Norwood Hamilton pattern. So the question is what causes that specific tension pattern? Well in my own observations (feeling peoples heads lol) the people with hair loss have very prominent temporal ridges and parietal eminences (where the temporalis attaches). And when your jaw is in a set back position it causes your temporalis to essentially be activated much much more during mastication which would cause those bones to grow via Wolfe’s law. That’s the initial hypothesis anyway.
